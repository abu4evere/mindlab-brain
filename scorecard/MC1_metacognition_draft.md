# MC1: does the brain know when it does not know? (DRAFT idea; not frozen, nothing built; for the creator and council)

**Why.** The mind-evidence baseline (`mind_evidence_MBb_2026-11-25.md`) found HOT-2 (metacognitive monitoring) absent.
It is also practical: a brain that can tell "I know where food is" from "I am guessing" should explore when unsure and
exploit when sure, which every study since W3c has needed. The creator asked (25 Nov) to keep working on the side of
measuring a mind.

**The probe (test-only, any kept brain):** on held-out lands, two matched states that look alike to the planner but
differ in how reliable its memory is (a food patch seen 5 steps ago vs one seen 900 steps ago, the staleness layer
being the only difference). A **safe option** is offered: a move that is worth a little for sure (a known small food
source nearby) against the larger but uncertain patch. Metacognition, read behaviourally (as in animal
"uncertainty-monitoring" tasks): the brain takes the safe option more often when its memory is stale, and its own
value spread (how uncertain its plan is) predicts that choice.

**What would count:** a brain whose choice of the safe option rises with staleness beyond what a staleness-only rule
predicts, with its own uncertainty signal carrying the effect (a lesion: clamp that signal and the effect goes).

**Open questions:** MoveVIN has no explicit uncertainty variable; would the study need one (an ensemble, or a learned
"how sure am I" output, the W3r/CX2 tables already count trials), and is that adding metacognition by hand?
