Mind Lab (a project building an autonomous, non-LLM artificial mind, "Bob") asks your critical review of a draft yardstick, the Bob Profile. Please answer as a critic: what is unfair, untestable, gameable or missing, and what you would change. The creator's goal: at least 60/100 overall and at least 10/20 in each ability. Five abilities, 20 points each, 20 = adult-human level on an equivalent test. Levels are anchored at 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20; a brain's score is the highest level whose frozen test passes (all lower levels too); untested shows as a dash. Each test has a positive control (a scripted agent built to have the ability must pass), a negative control (random must fail), and passes on the lower bound of an interval over at least 8 training seeds. Designer help is named: memory must be the brain's own above level 2; autonomy is tested with reflexes off above level 2. LEARNING: 2 beats random on held-out lands after training; 5 recovers to 80% within 300 steps after a lasting change (food patch moves, two foods swap value), no teacher; 10 learns a never-seen task within one life to 50% of a scripted solver in 3,000 steps; 15 within 2x human novice tries; 20 learning to learn at human level. REASONING: 2 detour to a goal 5+ steps behind one obstacle, better than greedy; 5 detours needing a 10+ step move away and choosing near-small vs far-large reward by value; 10 ordered sub-goals (key, door, food) on unseen layouts 70%; 15 inference from observation (which lever opens which door) and simple tool use; 20 human level. MEMORY: 2 returns to something seen 100+ steps ago now out of sight (any memory, even designer-written); 5 its own learned memory recalls after 1,000 steps of distraction 80%; 10 no catastrophic forgetting (A then B, still A at 80%, 10,000+ steps later); 15 episodic what-where-when used to plan; 20 human level. GENERALIZATION: 2 fresh lands keep 50% of training-land performance; 5 surface change with same rules (new colours, bigger map) keeps 80%; 10 changed causes (moving food, new hazard, other body costs) adapt within one life to 70% of a scripted agent, and above chance in a different kind of world without retraining; 15 skills carry into a very different world; 20 human level. AUTONOMY: 2 acts from its own needs and the choice tracks need sizes; 5 bare survival 50% on fresh lands and it prefers an informative observation over an equally new useless one; 10 an Unassigned Problem Test (notices an opportunity, forms a goal not among its built-in needs, plans, revises when blocked); 15 holds a project across interruptions, chooses sub-goals, turns away from a bad teacher; 20 human level. Today's brain (a value-iteration planner with drives, reflexes, a parent) is expected around 10. Questions: (1) Are the anchors well ordered and roughly equal steps across abilities? (2) Which tests could a narrow trick pass without the ability? (3) Is summing to 100 defensible, or should the 10-each floor dominate? (4) Is 20 = adult human a sensible anchor, and how would you set human anchors? (5) What would you change before freezing levels 2 and 5, which we build first in a 15x15 grid world with food, shelter, walls, energy and tiredness?
