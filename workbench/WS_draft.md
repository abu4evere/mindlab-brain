# WS: learning step-by-step skills fast. Which brain part lets a child copy a sequence after a few showings? (DRAFT, 13 November 2026)

**The creator** (13 November 2026, "ok go with your suggestion"): pause Cradle's copying rungs; build the brain part
that learns sequences, then return to Cradle's cumulative test.

**Why:** Cradle's C2-K and C2-K2 found the bottleneck.
- A child copying expert demonstrations could not learn a 2- or 3-step skill in 30 days.
- With 2 lids and 3 tries a day, copying everyone opened only 8% of boxes.

**Real infants do far better:**
- 14-month-olds reproduce novel acts a week after seeing them (Meltzoff 1988);
- 16-month-olds recall action sequences in the right order, especially when one step enables the next (Bauer & Mandler
  1989).

They remember the *whole demonstrated sequence* and replay it. Our child takes one small learning step per lid and
forgets the rest.

## The task (Cradle C2-K's boxes, as a workbench test)
- 6 kinds of box, each with **2 lids** (the C2-K2 L2 level: 1 try per kind per day), opened in order.
- **One reliable demonstrator only**, so this is about learning sequences, not whom to copy.
- Demonstrations on days 1–30; tests at days 1–60.
- Pilot levels: 2 lids, then 3 lids.

## The brain parts compared (all with C2's base network)
| brain | added part | the idea |
|---|---|---|
| **B0** | none (C2-K2's child) | baseline: one copy step per lid, at half rate |
| **Bc** | a stronger copy (full rate) | is it just the copy rate? |
| **Br** | **replay:** the last 20 demonstrations are kept in an episodic store, and each night the child rehearses each one as a copy step | the sleep-and-replay idea (hippocampal replay) |
| **Bm** | **a sequence memory:** a small recurrent state carries the demonstrated sequence through the try, so the child can reproduce lid 2 after lid 1 without the demonstration in view | does holding the sequence help? |
| **Brm** | replay and the sequence memory | both |

**In every brain, the demonstration of each lid is visible only before the try** (a showing, then the child acts from
memory), as in deferred imitation. In C2-K the pair stayed in view.

## Measures
- Full-box success at days 7, 30 and 60 (retention).
- The number of showings needed to reach 0.5.

## Readings (to be frozen)
1. **"Replay makes sequences learnable":** Br − B0 at day 30 has a lower bound above 0.
2. **"Memory helps reproduce the sequence":** Bm − B0 has a lower bound above 0.
3. **"Not just the copy rate":** Br − Bc has a lower bound above 0.

**Target:** a brain at 0.5 or more by day 30. Then Cradle's cumulative test (C2-K) can be rerun with it.

## Open questions for the council
1. Is nightly replay a fair "inborn mechanism", in the way the map memory was in W2?
2. Should the sequence memory be learned (recurrent) or a fixed buffer?
3. Is one demonstrator right, to isolate learning sequences from choosing whom to copy?

## The assistant's prior
- Replay helps: about 70%.
- Memory helps: about 50%.
- Replay beats a stronger copy: about 55%.
