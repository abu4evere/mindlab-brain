## 1. "That worked!" made the brain a better learner before the change, and much better fed
- **Group A at day 30 (still in the teaching), arm − Base:**
  - YS: +0.153 [+0.083, +0.229] at size 128; +0.132 [+0.076, +0.194] at size 32;
  - TBYS: +0.097 and +0.160.
- **Group B at day 45 (before its change), size 32:** YS +0.097 [+0.049, +0.153].
- **Lifetime food, arm − Base:**
  - YS +113 (size 128) and +155 (size 32);
  - **TBYS +230 [+205, +255] and +333 [+304, +363].** The small TBYS child earns 761 against Base's 428.
- **No harm before the change** for YS-128: B45 −0.028 [−0.076, +0.014]. The prior (about 70%): **yes**.

## 2. Relearning after a change: still almost nothing, a small gain with TBYS
- **Size 128, arm − Base:**
  - A45: YS −0.021, TBYS +0.007;
  - B60: YS +0.007, **TBYS +0.062 [+0.028, +0.104]** (absolute 0.06).
- **TBYS − TB (WM's TB children):**
  - A45 +0.076 [+0.035, +0.125]; B60 +0.049 [+0.021, +0.083] at size 128;
  - at size 32: +0.028 and +0.035.
  - So YS keeps a little of what TB's search finds.
- **The priors** (a gain of at least 0.15 on both A45 and B60: YS about 40%, TBYS about 55%): **no**.

## 3. The old habit got stronger
- **Stale habit at day 33** (group A, the old first-lid pair still chosen): Base 0.58, **YS 0.75, TBYS 0.81** (size
  128); 0.60, 0.76, 0.81 (size 32).
- By copying its own successes before the change, the brain wrote the old codes in deeper. After the change, there is
  more habit to undo.

## What it means (the assistant's reading)
- **"That worked!" is a real brain part:** strong learning from one's own surprising success. It speeds the first
  learning and feeds the child far better.
- **But relearning is blocked by habit, not by finding or by keeping.**
  - Search finds the new answer (TB), and "that worked!" writes it in (a little). Both fight a deep, slowly built
    habit with steps of a fixed small size (WS's step limit, CLIP).
- **Next candidate (the creator's choice): a fast memory beside the slow habit.**
  - Brains are thought to pair a fast memory that stores what just happened (the hippocampus) with a slow one that
    builds habits (the cortex). This is the "complementary learning systems" idea (McClelland and colleagues, 1995;
    from memory, not read today).
  - A fast memory of "what last worked on this box" could act at once after a change, while the habit slowly follows.
- **A design question for the creator:**
  - WM's and WY's tests count only what the slow connections hold;
  - if Bob's brain has a fast memory, the test should probably count what the whole brain does.

## Limits
