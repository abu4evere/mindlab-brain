Council on Mind Lab W3b (the same text to GPT, Gemini and DeepSeek). W3 (drives only: survival, comfort, curiosity; a V+ planner on a remembered 15x15 map; actor-critic learning from the drive rewards only; 2,000,000 steps per run, 8 seeds per brain) is done: no learned brain survived to 3,000 steps (mean lifespans 195-246 steps; random actions 174; a hand-written script that uses only the same map memory 2,963 and survives 0.96). Even D3i, which was given the designer's links (hunger -> the food channel, fatigue -> the shelter, curiosity -> uncertainty), died at 215. Curiosity did make brains explore (curiosity-only knew 0.86 of its land; D3 knew +0.10 more than the no-curiosity brain). Training lifespans rose only from about 170 to 205 in 2,000,000 steps (about 10,000 lives), so learning to act from sparse, delayed reward is far too slow, the same weakness the box tests met. The box tests found three fixes: "that worked!" (copying one's own surprising success at the full copy rate) speeds first learning and raises food; a fast store of what just worked, replayed at night into the slow network, gave the first real relearning after a change (0.45 against 0.06 after 14 days); acting directly from that store (engineered) relearned about 1.0 within a week. Proposed W3b, in W3's world with W3's drives and test (survival to 3,000 steps on the same 50 test lands, 8 seeds, paired by seed, on GitHub's cloud with each run split into chunks under 6 hours): step 0 = a read-only diagnostic of D3i's saved weights, tested two ways: (a) its learned action head as in W3, (b) walking greedily down the planner's own value map; if (b) lives, the head is the broken part, if (b) also dies, the planner's values are wrong (too short a horizon for food 15 cells away, or uncertainty swamping hunger). Then the arms: D3 (W3's, the reference); D3+Y ("that worked!": after a big unexpected drop in the drive, such as eating when hungry or resting when exhausted, the last N moves are copied as a self-imitation step, weighted by the positive surprise); D3+F (a fast place memory: where each need was last relieved; when that need is high and the place is known, the child walks there; otherwise the learned brain acts; an engineered ceiling, labelled); D3+FR (the store only teaches: at rest in the shelter the stored successful paths are replayed into the slow network); D3+Y+FR (both). Questions, please answer briefly point by point: 1) Is step 0 worth doing first, and is the greedy value-map read-out the right diagnostic? 2) Is D3+F acceptable as a labelled ceiling under the project's rule "program the body, not the decisions"? 3) Should the test stay at 3,000 steps or first ask a smaller thing (survive 1,000 steps)? 4) Define Y precisely for a step-by-step life: what counts as a surprising drop, how many moves N to copy, and with what weight. 5) Define FR precisely: what is stored (a path or a place), when it is replayed, and how many replay steps. 6) Should W3's engineered map memory fade (the project's leaky-memory lesson) or stay perfect for now? 7) What would make the result uninterpretable, and how to prevent it? 8) Your estimate: the chance that any learned W3b arm (not D3+F) reaches a survival share of 0.5 or more.
