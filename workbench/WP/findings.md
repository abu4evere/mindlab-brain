## 1. Watching each other made the children worse at the skill of their own, but better fed
**Stable world, own skill at day 60 (mean of children 1–5):**

| group | own skill, day 60 | food per child |
|---|---|---|
| S6-alone (never watch each other) | 0.399 | 615 |
| S6 (six small, watching) | 0.247 | 656 |
| B1S5 (one big, five small) | 0.376 | 692 (small); big child 1031 |
| B6 (six big, watching) | 0.135 | 1220 |

- **S6 − S6-alone (small children 1–5): −0.152 [−0.182, −0.123] at day 60.**
  The prior ("watching helps", about 70%): **no**, the reverse.
- **Six big brains watching each other** have the lowest own skill (0.13 at day 60), yet the most food (1220 per
  child).
- **The same parrot as in WN and WE.** The other children are always there, and each one's latest try is held in
  memory just before trying.
  - This makes the peers a teacher who never leaves.
  - Food comes from replaying what someone just did; the skill of one's own does not grow.
  - (A reading, consistent with WN, WE and WT; not a tested cause.)

## 2. One big child among small ones helped them, compared with six small
- **Small children 1–5, B1S5 − S6:** day 60 **+0.128 [+0.098, +0.158]**; day 30 +0.002.
  The prior (about 45%): **yes**.
- But B1S5's small children (0.376) only got back to about where children alone are (0.399).
- **The big child:** B1S5 child 0 at day 60, 0.309, against 0.104 in B6 (+0.205 [+0.153, +0.257]). A big brain among
  small ones keeps more skill of its own than among big ones.
- **Whom they watch:** the small children gave the big child 0.278 [0.266, 0.291] of their watching on days 1–30,
  and 0.289 on days 31–60, against 0.20 by chance.
  - A mild preference. The prior ("above 0.30", about 55%): **no**.

## 3. Changing world: watching each other did not spread new codes
- Everyone falls to near 0 after the changes, as in WC.
- Relearning (group B, day 60) is 0.006–0.033 in every group type.
- **B1S5 − S6 (small, group B day 60):** +0.003 [0.000, +0.007]. The prior (about 35%): **no** useful effect.
- **Nobody finds the new codes alone (WC, WM),** so there is nothing to spread. A group cannot copy a discovery
  nobody made.

## What it means (the assistant's reading)
- **A brain that can hold someone's latest try in perfect memory becomes a replayer whenever models are always
  available.** It does not matter whether the model is a grown-up or a peer.
- This is the creator's point of 14 November ("humans cannot be parrot like agents. Our brain cannot easily remember
  things"). WL tests it directly with a leaky memory.
- **Groups can only build on what someone finds.** The relearning gap (WC, WM; WY under test) comes first.

## Limits
- Trust only from seen success; copying only the lids seen to open.
- Peers always visible; no cost for neurons; one small task.
