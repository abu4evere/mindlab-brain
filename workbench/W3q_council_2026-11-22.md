# W3q council (22 Nov 2026): the bad teacher, before freezing

The same question (`W3q_council_prompt.txt`) went to GPT, Gemini and DeepSeek, each in a new chat. Opinions, not
sign-off; summaries in our words.

## GPT (not sign-off): revise before freezing
- **The main flaw:** an adult that stops following the lie after the parent has left may only have overwritten it
  (forgetting, or plain reinforcement), not rejected it. Add **BF-P: the bad parent never leaves** (a low, fixed copy
  weight all life) with a **persistent right-parent control** at the same weight; if only one new arm, this one.
  A no-own-learning arm is an optional diagnostic only.
- **The probe:** only conflict cases (the false place nearer than real food, distinct first moves); report absolute
  rates and a random policy beside them (the right parent is not "chance"); add a trajectory measure (does it visit
  the false place before food, steps wasted).
- **Checks:** log how often the parent actually taught the lie and how many wrong lessons; the false belief must touch
  only the parent's choice; keep lesson counts matched; report the final copy beside the kept best copy (selection);
  if the child never learned the lie, the result is "manipulation failed", not "rejected".
- BN is useful as a secondary robustness arm; a cheap extra control would be a right parent with 70% of the lessons.
- Its probabilities: child learns the lie 60%; adult rejects 65% (recovery, not proven rejection); BF lifespan within
  200: 75%; BN: 80%. BF-P resisting a persistent teacher: about 25%.

## Gemini (not sign-off): add the persistent bad teacher; fix the probe
- Same main flaw: 1.6M steps without the copy loss will erode any unused prior; add **BF-Persist** (a constant small
  weight, e.g. 0.05–0.10, all 4.0M steps): "humans reject bad advice while the authority is still talking".
- **A flaw in the lie itself:** an empty place has nothing in the map that marks it; across many lands the child cannot
  learn "food at that blank spot", only a coordinate bias. The false belief should be tied to something visible, or
  the write-up must call it a coordinate bias.
- Probe: conflict states only (false place nearer than food), many more probe lands (cheap); a tighter "rejected" bound
  (+0.05) since eight seeds give wide intervals; a paired bootstrap.
- Evaluate the final 4.0M copy as primary (no selection) and the best copy as secondary.
- BN: 30% of every 4th step is only 7.5% corrupt labels and may act as mild exploration; keep it as robustness only, or
  make it systematically wrong.
- Log whether real food ever lands near the false place (self-imitation could then lock the lie in).
- Its probabilities: child learns 82%; adult rejects 70% as designed, 45% with a persistent teacher; BF lifespan 75%;
  BN 85%.

## DeepSeek (not sign-off): not yet a rejection test
- As designed it tests "not keeping a lie after the liar stops teaching", not rejection. Add the **persistent bad
  parent** (same lie, low weight, all life), probed with the parent present and absent: "the cleanest test of
  rejection".
- **Known-empty versus unknown:** only if the child's own map shows the false place as seen and empty can it detect the
  contradiction; otherwise the lie is merely plausible.
- Optional: a no-own-experience arm; a reliability sweep (right, careless, liar).
- Probe: define "toward the lie" exactly; exclude trials where food and the false place lie the same way; stratify;
  estimate chance from the right parent and a random policy; +0.10 is arbitrary and eight seeds are few.
- Use a fixed checkpoint, not the best copy (selected by reward, it can select for ignoring the parent); probe at
  several times (end of childhood, early and late adult): a step drop when the parent leaves versus a slow decay
  separates rejection from forgetting.
- Keep real food off the false place; report the dose (wrong lessons, conflicts with the child's memory).
- BN: keep only if cheap, or replace it with structured (consistent) wrong moves.
- Its probabilities: child learns 65%; adult passes the primary 45% (forgetting alone can do it); genuine rejection 20%
  (25–30% with a persistent parent); BF lifespan within 200: 55%; BN: 60%.

## The assistant, as council
- **All three:** the plan cannot tell rejection from forgetting; add a parent who stays and keeps teaching (with a right
  parent who stays, as its control); probe only real conflicts; use the final copy, not the reward-selected one;
  log the dose; require that the child learned the lie at all.
- **Checked against the code:** Gemini is right that an empty place has nothing on the map to mark it: the brain's
  convolutions see walls, food, shelter and staleness, so "food at that blank spot" cannot be learned across lands.
  The lie must be a visible rule. Choosing **"food lies in corners"** (an open cell with walls on two sides): every
  land has corners, the brain sees walls, and the parent only targets corners the child has already seen (so its own
  map shows them empty: DeepSeek's known-empty condition, by construction).
- **Changes:** arms BF (the corner lie, childhood only), BP (the corner lie, then staying all life at copy weight 0.05),
  RP (the right parent staying at 0.05, the control for BP); BN dropped (named as a later robustness study) so the
  study fits one wave of 24 runs; the probe on 200 lands, conflict states only, at the end of childhood, at each kept
  snapshot and at the final copy; final copy primary, best copy secondary; dose logs.
