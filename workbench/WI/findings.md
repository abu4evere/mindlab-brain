# WI findings: imagination in the silent room (exploratory; numbers from `analyse_wi.py`)

Plan: `questions.md` (WI-frozen). Checks All: PASS before data. Run 16 November 2026 on GitHub's machines (2 jobs, 16
files). n = 8 seeds per arm, paired. Mind Lab: exploratory, no council, no blind recompute.

## The readings
1. **"Imagination changes life in the silent room": no, hardly.** IM − L in the last window:
   - activity +0.018 [+0.002, +0.039];
   - reach −0.4 cells [−11.9, +13.4];
   - pattern −0.005 [−0.014, +0.001].
   
   With dreams or without, the recipe brain still moved in a small, repeated circuit (reach about 25 cells per 1,000
   steps).
2. **"Dreams keep the skill alive": no difference.** After the room, on W3's 50 test lands:
   - lifespan Pre 312, L 298, IM 295;
   - IM − L −3.3 [−17.2, +6.1]; IM − Pre −17.4 [−49.6, +5.4];
   - survival 0.013, 0.007 and 0.015, all near 0.
   
   Ten thousand steps in the silent room, with or without dreams, neither harmed nor helped the skill clearly.
3. **"Dreams get better": yes, a little.** Food eaten per dream rose from 5.0 (first 5 dreams) to 6.2 (last 5): +1.2
   [+0.03, +2.4].

**The priors:**
- IM has more reach and less pattern (about 55%): **no.**
- IM lives longer than L after (about 55%): **no.** Longer than Pre (about 40%): **no.**
- Dreams get better (about 60%): **yes.**

## What it means (the assistant's reading)
- **The imagination as built is practice in the mind: the brain got slightly better at its dreams.**
  - But dreaming did not change what it did while awake in the silent room, and it did not change its skill in the
    real world.
  - Nineteen dreams of 200 steps (3,800 imagined steps) are small against the 2,000,000 steps it was trained on.
- **More importantly, nothing in this brain lets a dream reach waking life.**
  - A human who imagines a game then plays it: the imagined thing becomes a goal.
  - Here, imagined food leaves no goal behind. The waking brain still acts only on what it sees and on curiosity's
    staleness.
- **So the creator's question ("will it invent its own activity in silence?") still gets "not yet."** The missing link
  is **imagination that sets goals**: dreaming of something, then going to look for it or trying it while awake.
- **Next candidates (the creator's choice):**
  - (1) dreams that leave goals (an imagined place or act becomes something the waking brain seeks);
  - (2) the "getting-better" curiosity (wanting what one is still learning);
  - (3) longer dreaming (a dose test), less likely to change the conclusion.

## Limits
- Engineered imagination (a world simulated from the brain's own map memory); one room; 8 seeds; fixed dream length
  and frequency.
