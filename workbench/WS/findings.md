# WS findings: learning step-by-step skills fast (14 November 2026)

**Frozen plan:** `questions.md` (WS-frozen). **Run:** local, 384 children (8 brains × 48 seeds). Checks All: PASS.
**Frozen analysis:** `output/results.md`. A Mind Lab workbench study, so no blind recompute (per the 13 November
handoff, recomputes are for ACL and Cradle). n = 48 per brain.

## The frozen readings (day 30)
1. **PRIMARY. "Replaying showings helps, beyond the amount of copying": no.** Br − Bu +0.014 [−0.021, +0.049].
   Nightly replay is just more copying: it equals the same extra copy done at once.
2. **"A learned memory helps the child copy a sequence": yes, strongly.** After a showing, Bm − B0 +0.486 [+0.417,
   +0.556]. Bm opens 0.913 of the boxes just shown to it, against B0's 0.427.
3. **"Both together beat either": no.** Brm − max(Br, Bm) −0.212 [−0.285, −0.139] (skill at day 30).
4. **"Replay beats the baseline": yes.** Br − B0 +0.101 [+0.059, +0.142]. That is the extra copy amount (Bu − B0
   +0.087).

## What else the data show (reported checks; descriptive)
- **The copy rate matters most for skill by day 30:** Bc (full rate) 0.858, against B0 0.448.
- **Memory changes how the child learns:**
  - **while showings last** (days 1–30), the memory child leans on them. Its skill with no showing is lower at day 30
    (Bm 0.351 against B0 0.448), but shown a box, it nearly always does it right (0.913);
  - **once showings stop, it learns fast by itself:** skill at day 60 is 0.920 (Brm 0.958), against B0 0.542. Its
    retention gain (day 60 − day 30) is +0.569 [+0.500, +0.635].

  This is like a toddler who first imitates what they were just shown, then makes it their own by practising.
- **Learning is specific to the right sequence:** with random showings (Bx), skill stays near 0 (0.017).
- **Reward learning matters for keeping the skill:** copy only (Bp) loses a little after day 30 (0.444 → 0.427),
  while B0 keeps improving.

## An honest correction to Cradle's C2-K2
- **Here the baseline (B0) reaches 0.448 at day 30.** In C2-K2's easiest level, copying everyone reached only 0.076.
- **The differences:** WS has one reliable demonstrator, and every showing is copied before the try. In C2-K and C2-K2,
  half the showings came from a non-expert, and lid 2 was copied only when the child reached it.
- **So C2-K2's floor was partly the design** (gated copying and unreliable demonstrators), not only "the brain learns
  sequences too slowly", as C2-K2's findings said. A correction is appended there.

## For the brain (`brain_v1_plan.md`)
- **A learned recurrent memory of what was just shown** is a part to keep: it gives deferred imitation (the infants'
  ability: Meltzoff 1988; Bauer & Mandler 1989), and it speeds lasting skill once teaching stops.
- **Nightly replay adds nothing beyond its copy amount;** the copy rate itself matters.
