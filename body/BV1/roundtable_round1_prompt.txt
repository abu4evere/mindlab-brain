Round-table, round 1 (Mind Lab, Body v1 step 1, 'BV1'; a draft, not frozen). Context: Mind Lab trains small neural brains on grid lands (walls, food patches, one shelter; hunger and tiredness; lives up to 3,000 steps; tested on fresh lands). The body has 5 actions (4 moves and stay); eating and resting are automatic (standing on food when not full; standing at the shelter). The candidate brain (W3r's 'RM' brains, used only if W3r passes; its result is due 27 Nov) sees raw colour codes and learns within each life what each colour does from three body consequences (blocked, fed, rested), in a table that feeds the brain's wall, food and shelter layers. Body v1 is a standard interface meant for any world: eyes (raw looks, never labels), touch, hands, feelings (hunger, thirst, tiredness, pain), signed feedback after each action, self; verbs move, rest, use, operate(k) (the world's own operations as shuffled numbers); a per-world adapter translates formats and judges legality, and never labels meaning or chooses for the brain. BV1 tests the frozen brain through Body v1 with no change to the brain: arms NATIVE (the brain's own wiring), BODY (through Body v1; the table learns from Body v1's feedback: blocked = place unchanged after a move, fed = hunger fell, rested = tiredness fell), BODY-SHUF (colour codes and verb numbers shuffled per land), BODY-PAD (8 dummy operations that do nothing, random order); 16 seeds, frozen brains, the same fresh test lands. Primary: non-inferiority of BODY vs NATIVE, paired by seed (the lower 95% bound of the mean-lifespan difference above -5% of NATIVE's mean; of the full-life survival difference above -3 points; the worst quarter of seeds not more than 10% worse). Checks before data: a zero check (BODY reproduces NATIVE bit for bit on 2 seeds) and an adapter audit as its own gate before any brain is attached (no adapter output, such as legality, availability, ordering, auto-targeting or hidden state, correlated with reward-relevant kinds across renamings). In this world there are no operations (K = 0) and hands are always empty, so 'use' always does nothing. Questions: (1) If the adapter reproduces the native run bit for bit, is the non-inferiority run still worth doing, or should step 1 be just the zero check plus the audits, with the data budget moved to step 2 (a small crafting world where things held in the hands combine)? (2) Is 'hands empty, use does nothing, K = 0' a fair first test of the body, or does it test too little? (3) If W3r fails (the brain cannot learn colour meanings within a life), what should step 1 be? (4) Are the margins right for a frozen-brain, connection-only test? Please answer each question briefly with your reasons, give your overall recommendation, and say how sure you are (a percentage).
